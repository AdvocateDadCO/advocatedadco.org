Special education / Federal advocacy · January 2026

National IDEA advocacy — U.S. Department of Education / OSERS

Rob Harris participated as a Colorado parent advocate in the January 2026 U.S. Department of Education Office of Special Education and Rehabilitative Services (OSERS) listening session on special education and vocational rehabilitation.

His comments on accountability under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA), state-to-state inconsistency, and the burden placed on families were featured in the Advance IDEA Coalition’s national response. The response was published and amplified across national disability and special-education organizations. The coalition includes the Council of Parent Attorneys and Advocates (COPAA), Council for Exceptional Children (CEC), National Center for Learning Disabilities (NCLD), The Arc of the United States, and the Center for Learner Equity.

Rob’s remarks connected federal oversight to what families experience: uneven access to services, the expectation that parents master complex systems, and the burden that falls on families when those systems fail—especially for students with low-incidence disabilities such as blindness.

For the fall 2026 Washington advocacy work, that broader record supports two focused Congressional asks: protect the entire Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA)—including Part D—in final fiscal year 2027 appropriations, and support and advance Senate bill 5046 (S.5046) so the Office of Special Education and Rehabilitative Services (OSERS) and other core education functions remain within the U.S. Department of Education. Rob’s role is to show why that federal infrastructure matters when rural communities cannot recreate scarce specialized expertise on their own.

The method

Stories identify the harm. Evidence shows the pattern. Action changes the outcome.

Rob approaches advocacy like a systems problem: understand the rule, document what happened, identify the gap between policy and practice, ask direct questions, and keep the person affected at the center.

The goal is never attention for its own sake. The goal is a path that works better for the next family.
