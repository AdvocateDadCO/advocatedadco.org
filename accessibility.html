Accessibility

Access is not an afterthought.

A website advocating for blind, low-vision, and disabled people must hold itself to the same standard it asks of others.

Our commitment

Accessibility is a continuing practice.

AdvocateDadCO is designed to support keyboard navigation, screen readers, text enlargement, high-contrast settings, reduced-motion preferences, and mobile access.

We aim to meet or exceed the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2, Level AA, where applicable. We also recognize that conformance checks do not replace testing by disabled people using their own tools and strategies.

Current features

Built for more than one way of accessing information.

  • Semantic headings, regions, navigation, lists, quotations, and buttons
  • A skip link for keyboard and screen-reader users
  • Visible keyboard focus indicators
  • Descriptive alternative text for meaningful photographs
  • Decorative images hidden from assistive technology
  • Responsive layouts that support mobile devices and text enlargement
  • High-contrast colors and forced-colors support
  • Reduced-motion support
  • Plain-language link and button labels
  • Printable PDF resources accompanied by equivalent HTML information

Testing

Automated checks are the beginning.

The site is reviewed for keyboard operation, heading structure, focus visibility, color contrast, responsive layout, and screen-reader compatibility. Lived-user testing—including review with VoiceOver and by Isabelle—is part of the continuing improvement process.

Third-party platforms linked from this site may have their own accessibility barriers. Please report those experiences so we can provide another route when possible.

Found a barrier?

Tell us what did not work.

Include the page, what you were trying to do, and the browser or assistive technology you were using. We will treat the report as useful evidence—not an inconvenience.
