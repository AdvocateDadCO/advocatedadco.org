This Holiday Season, Support the Parents, Too

A note to parents of children with disabilities, and to the people who want to show up for them.

By Rob Harris · #AdvocateDad · November 22, 2026

All blog articles

As a dad, I’ve spent years asking what a school decision would mean for my daughter and what we’d need to do next. Those questions can follow you home, right into the time you set aside for your family.

With the holidays coming up, I’m thinking about the moms, dads, and caregivers raising kids who are blind, have low vision, or have other disabilities. There’s a lot happening in special education right now. The school emails and the worry about what comes next can make it hard to be present for the parts of the season you were looking forward to.

To the parents carrying all of this

Find your peace in the middle of all this. You can stay informed and still put the phone down. You don’t have to follow every policy change, learn federal education law over the holidays, or fight every battle yourself.

And please remember something. You’re doing a great job. Showing up, asking questions, and loving your child matters. Give yourself some room to enjoy being together. If you need help, reach out to another parent, an advocate, or an organization you trust. We can keep pushing for our kids’ rights and share the work.

If you want to help, offer something specific

If you’re a friend or relative, offer something specific. Bring dinner. Pick up groceries. Make room for the routines and access needs that help the whole family take part in a holiday gathering. Ask what would make the day easier, listen to the answer, and follow through.

Educators and advocates can help, too. Pass along one clear update a parent can use. Offer to talk through a school letter when they have time. A little less for a parent to sort through on their own can mean a lot.

Two resources from the American Foundation for the Blind

The American Foundation for the Blind is an organization I trust and respect. Save these for when you have the time.

Read — Taking Action to Respond to Cuts to the U.S. Department of Education

AFB explains why federal oversight matters to students with disabilities and offers practical ways for families and advocates to make their voices heard.

Read AFB’s article on responding to Department of Education cuts

Watch — IDEA at 50: Celebrating Progress in the Blindness Field and Beyond

In this recorded webinar, panelists from the blindness field look back at what the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) made possible and discuss why protecting that progress matters for students and families.

Watch the IDEA at 50 webinar on YouTube

Parents, take care of yourselves, too. Your well-being matters. And if you know a parent carrying a lot this season, check in and offer to take one thing off their plate.
