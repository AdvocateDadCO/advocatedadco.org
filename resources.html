Short demonstrations of accessible math workflows

CATT’s Tech in 20 series provides 20-minute demonstrations of math access using assistive technology from APH. Topics include Desmos with JAWS, accessible math materials, tactile graphics, and Monarch-ready charts and graphs.

Find Tech in 20 sessions, recordings, and math resources

Free and open to all. Register through the provider for live sessions and the full session-resource document. The series does not offer continuing education units (CEUs).

Science, technology, engineering, and math

The universe is for everyone.

Explore resources discussed in Dr. Tiffany Wild’s “Shooting for the Stars: Making Science Accessible” session. Use them to plan hands-on participation, usable information, and meaningful roles in science.

Astronomy, space, and tactile models

  • See3D — explore 3D models, model requests, and accessible 3D-printing work.
  • You Can Do Astronomy — Noreen Grice’s accessible astronomy books, tactile products, and workshops. Some offerings are paid.
  • Chandra: From Earth to the Universe tactile and Braille posters — astronomy materials and accompanying information.
  • Ohio State’s STEM Career Exploration Lab project — background on accessible astronomy, tactile models, and STEM career exploration; not a current enrollment announcement.
  • AstroAccess — disability inclusion and accessibility research in space exploration; check the organization for current activities.

Hands-on science and geoscience

  • Mission INSPIRE: hands-on rocket activity — an inquiry-based activity for grades 5–10 from Paths to Literacy.
  • International Association for Geoscience Diversity resources — materials for accessible curricula and participation in geoscience.
  • Topographic model tutorial on Instructables — a construction idea referenced in the handout; adapt materials and supervision to the learner.

Chemistry, lab access, and teaching strategies

  • Perkins: teaching chemistry — ideas for accessible chemistry instruction.
  • Perkins: acid-base titration using nonvisual tools — a specific lab-access example with demonstrations.
  • University of Washington DO-IT: making science labs accessible — planning guidance for lab environments, materials, and participation.
  • APH Press: accessible science teaching book edited by Tiffany Wild — a paid professional resource; check formats and pricing with APH.

Plan tools, instruction, communication, and safety with the student and qualified team before a lab or construction activity. Check older resources for changed equipment and links. Start with the scientific concept and the student’s participation; choose access methods that let the student investigate and demonstrate learning.

Conferences, meetings, and community events

Plan for participation before the invitation goes out.

INOVA accessible scientific meetings and conferences toolkit

A free planning resource developed by the Johns Hopkins Disability Health Research Center and the International Network of Researchers with Visual Impairments and their Allies (INOVA). It offers guidance for organizers, staff, presenters, and attendees, including planning before an event, access during sessions, and follow-up afterward.

Use it as a starting point when planning a conference, school meeting, church event, or community gathering. Consider registration, venue access, usable materials, presentations, and how people will request and receive support.

Get the INOVA toolkit and one-page flyers from Johns Hopkins — English, Spanish, German, and French editions are listed on the provider’s page.

Trusted resource · Literacy and music

Library of Congress: National Library Service (NLS)

Free braille and audiobook library services for eligible readers, including people who are blind or have low vision and people with qualifying print disabilities. During our Washington, D.C., advocacy trip, Isabelle and I experienced a custom tour with tactile access and met with NLS leadership. These resources bring reading, learning, and music into everyday life.

  • Read what interests you. Borrow books and magazines through the NLS library network, receive materials by mail, or download available titles through BARD. Equipment loans are available for using NLS materials.
  • Keep a favorite braille book. Braille on Demand lets registered patrons request up to five existing NLS braille titles each month in hardcopy to keep for personal use. It does not provide custom transcription of any book.
  • Make music with friends. Explore braille and large-print scores, audio instrument lessons, music theory, and popular-song lead sheets. Ask the Music Section about the piece your choir, teacher, or friends are using.
  • Find a music transcriber. NLS provides a directory of certified braille music transcribers. Custom work and any fees are arranged directly with the transcriber.

Explore NLS Music Service and Materials · Request Braille on Demand books · Learn about NLS and enrollment

Already an NLS patron? Contact the Music Section at nlsm@loc.gov or 800-424-8567, extension 2. Music services are requested directly from NLS.
