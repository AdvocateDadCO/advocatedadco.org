Why we are going

Policy needs the truth only lived experience can carry.

Washington, D.C. advocacyFall 2026

Rob and Isabelle will bring family, rural-implementation, and lived-experience perspectives to a federal education advocacy delegation.

The public agenda is intentionally narrow: two issues and two asks. Travel plans, meeting schedules, participant information, and how individual conversations were arranged will remain private unless the people involved choose to make them public.

The Congressional agenda

Two issues. Two asks.

The broader work provides evidence. These are the two federal actions Rob and Isabelle are asking Congress to take.

  1. 01

    Protect the entire Individuals with Disabilities Education Act—including Part D

    Protect funding for every part of the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) in final fiscal year 2027 appropriations. Preserve Part D national activities that sustain personnel preparation, technical assistance, parent training and family information, accessible technology and materials, and specialized expertise. Part B helps schools provide services; Part D helps build the people, knowledge, and capacity needed to provide them well.

  2. 02

    Support and advance Senate bill 5046

    Keep the Office of Special Education and Rehabilitative Services (OSERS) and other core education functions within the U.S. Department of Education. Monitoring of the federal special education law, technical assistance, coordination, and specialized expertise should remain connected to the broader education system—not fragmented or moved into a medical-services frame.

    Read Senate bill 5046 (S.5046) on Congress.gov

Why Isabelle’s presence matters

She is not going as a symbol.

Isabelle is participating as a blind young-adult self-advocate, Braille reader, assistive-technology user, and white-cane traveler. She can explain in her own words what specialized instruction, accessible technology, mobility, and educational access mean in a young person’s life.

Rob’s role is different and complementary: connect federal policy and infrastructure to family, rural, and implementation realities without speaking over Isabelle’s own voice.

The AdvocateDadCO foundation

Three commitments guide the broader work.

These are AdvocateDadCO’s permanent systems-change priorities. They shape work in Colorado and nationally, but they are not three additional Congressional asks for this D.C. delegation.

  1. 01

    Deliver access from day one

    Make timely Braille, accessible instructional materials, assistive technology, orientation and mobility (O&M), and Expanded Core Curriculum instruction an expectation—not an exception families must repeatedly fight to obtain.

  2. 02

    Build and support the workforce

    Strengthen the pipeline of teachers of students with visual impairments (TVIs), certified orientation and mobility specialists (COMS), interveners, and related professionals, with specific solutions for rural communities where distance and shortages compound inequity.

  3. 03

    Share power with the people affected

    Include disabled people and families meaningfully in policy design, implementation, evaluation, and oversight—not only after decisions have already been made.
